# ALPHA SOURCES — where to look, and the conventions that make a hypothesis worth a ticket (binding for Scout; AGENTS.md §3 points here)

Alpha is a predictable gap between price and value created by someone who has to trade, wants to trade for a non-price reason, or is systematically wrong. Start from the trader, never from the indicator. Every experiment names the family it works in (the idea-map cell's `mechanism_family`) and the counterparty it exploits.

## The eight families, with the questions that generate hypotheses

**A. Forced and mechanical flows** — participants who must transact regardless of price. Liquidation cascades and the deleveraging after them (open interest drops with volume spikes; who is left holding what?). Funding-rate mechanics (extreme funding forces position closure; funding-interval changes are exchange-declared stress). Index and basket rebalances; listing and delisting flows (new perps bleed, delistings force closes); token unlocks and vesting cliffs; treasury and foundation sales; staking/unstaking windows; collateral-haircut changes; insurance-fund and auto-deleveraging events; large-holder distribution visible in trade-size buckets. Ask: who is forced, when, in which names, how large relative to daily volume, and how long does the market take to absorb it?

**B. Positioning and leverage** — open interest versus volume (crowding), long/short account and position ratios, top-trader versus crowd positioning, leverage build-ups and unwinds, the premium index as the price of leverage (unprovisioned today), basis between venues. Distinguish price moves caused by information from moves caused by positioning: the second reverts, the first does not. Ask: is the crowd long or short, how levered, and what happens when it is wrong?

**C. Market microstructure** — the 1-minute data makes this testable. Taker buy/sell imbalance at minute-to-hour scales, trade-size distributions (retail versus institutional prints), spread proxies from high/low ranges, volume clustering, intraday seasonality by UTC hour and around funding prints, liquidity withdrawal before large moves, temporary versus permanent impact, lead/lag between large and small names, volume-at-price concentration. Ask: does an intraday signature predict the next hours or days, and does it survive the cost of trading it? (Known: hourly taker flow reverses at every horizon with execution-level value under 2 bp — any intraday claim must clear costs explicitly.) The bucket now carries daily taker flow split by print size — buy/sell $ and print counts in six buckets from under $100 to $1m+, per symbol-day since 2020 (`taker_flow/interval=1d`, see the `binance-futures-data` skill) — so trade-size-distribution hypotheses are measurable at the daily horizon; the config spec exposes only the day's total flow fields so far, so a ranking that needs the size split starts as a data-request escalation naming the `taker_flow` columns.

**D. Behavioural** — momentum and reversal at every horizon (on this universe weekly cross-sectional trend REVERSES; six tests), under- and over-reaction to large moves, attention spikes (volume without price), anchoring to round numbers and recent highs, disposition effects after drawdowns, panic days (forced sellers hit liquid names first — real, measured, +180 bp per 3 days in the liquid tercile), weekend and holiday effects. Ask: which participants are slow or emotional, and in which names?

**E. Carry and risk premia** — funding, basis, term structure, volatility risk premium, borrow and staking yields. Separate the premium (compensated risk) from the alpha (mispricing): a carry that pays in calm and loses it all in a squeeze is a risk premium with a shape, not an edge. The production funding-carry sleeve already harvests the funding sort with a trend gate; what is not yet understood is its decomposition (funding yield + spot/perp convergence + directional + selection − costs). Ask: who pays the carry and why, and when do they stop?

**F. Relative value** — pairs within declared sectors (L1s, L2s, memecoins, DeFi, AI), peer-relative moves, dispersion, cross-venue price and funding gaps (Binance vs Bybit vs OKX vs Hyperliquid), spot-perp basis, capital-structure relationships between a protocol's token and its ecosystem. Prefer "A beats B" to "A goes up". Ask: what should move together, and what breaks the link temporarily? (Known: price-derived clusters and cross-venue funding gaps add nothing at the weekly horizon; declared sector tags are not yet provisioned.)

**G. Cross-market information** — does one market lead another: BTC → alts, large → small, options (DVOL, skew) → spot, stablecoin flows → risk appetite, equities and rates → crypto beta, on-chain activity → exchange prices, news and listings → price. Ask: who sees the information first, and how long is the lag? (Known: BTC-dominance regime timing is dead after six tests; alt catch-up to BTC shocks is ~15 bp per 4 h, below costs.)

**H. The production book's own failures** — the richest source for a mature book. Where does it lose: by sleeve, long versus short, liquidity tier, volatility and dispersion regime, trend regime, funding environment, open-interest regime, time since entry, signal strength? Clusters of losses with a common, pre-entry-observable cause become conditioned frameworks (a ranking switched on only in the state where the mechanism should hold, via `gt`/`lt` + `where`). Ask: what distinguishes the losing trades using only what was known at entry? Never fit a filter to the losers — Judge's test decides.

## Conventions a hypothesis must satisfy to be worth a ticket
1. **A mechanism with a counterparty** — who is on the other side and why they behave predictably; "it might work" is not a mechanism.
2. **Direction and horizon from the mechanism** — the holding horizon follows the decay you expect, never the lookback that maximised something.
3. **A point-in-time observable** — expressible with the data fields and operations of the `trade-config-spec` skill (every field is already lagged for you); lookback ≤ 180 days. An intraday idea must be expressed through the daily aggregates the spec exposes or escalated as a data request.
4. **Dollars, not ranks** — say why the long-minus-short spread survives the funding each leg pays and the cost of rebalancing at the horizon (`WHAT_FAILED_BEFORE.md` pattern 1).
5. **A kill result** — what Judge's result would have to show for you to abandon the mechanism (wrong sign, early-only, costs, placebo), and the base case you expect (usually FAIL) — written in the ticket's Expectation paragraph.
6. **Not a costume** — name the nearest ledger experiment and the closest known sleeve (funding sort, illiquidity/size, 7-day taker flow — `GLOSSARY.md`) and why this differs; a ranking that is one of them in disguise is a re-run. Say which pattern in `WHAT_FAILED_BEFORE.md` it most risks and why it escapes.
7. **Capacity** — how many names carry the signal at once and whether they are liquid enough to trade; a signal living only in the thinnest names is a note, not a proposal; holds shorter than a day are not expressible.
8. **Simple first** — one ranking, top-vs-bottom selection, equal weight; one rolling sum beats a pairwise loop; complexity must earn its place in a later framework round, not in round 1.
9. **Relative over directional**; **unvisited families over crowded ones** (a family with no experiment in 30 days outranks another visit to a busy one); **strong tests over weak ones** (prefer a hypothesis Judge can confirm or reject decisively).

## Reading external sources (papers, posts, podcasts, datasets)
Extract every claim that maps onto a mechanism and a buildable feature; ignore the headline numbers (published crypto "factors" have repeatedly been ranks-without-dollars or the funding sort relabelled). Map each claim onto its idea-map cell and the observable it implies; propose from it only through a normal fresh run. A claim that needs a field the spec does not carry becomes an escalation requesting the data, not a proposal.
